"How do I do the refund again?" "Where's the supplier login?" "What do we do if the delivery's late?" If these questions come up every week, the problem isn't your team. It's that the knowledge they need lives in people's heads instead of somewhere they can find it.

The warning signs

  • The same questions keep coming to the same one or two people, usually including you.
  • New starters take weeks to become useful, and they learn by shadowing whoever has time.
  • The same task is done slightly differently depending on who does it.
  • Mistakes repeat, because the lesson from last time was never captured.
  • You hesitate to take a holiday, because things go wrong when you're not there.
  • There are written procedures somewhere, but they're out of date and nobody opens them.

Why it matters more than it seems

Your best people become a help desk

Every question is a small interruption. Across a week, the people who know the most spend a large part of their time answering instead of doing, and the business slows down to the pace of their availability.

Quality depends on who's on shift

When there's no agreed way of doing something, customers get a different experience depending on who serves them. Customers notice when the service changes from one visit to the next, and a consistent service is hard to deliver without a shared standard.

Knowledge walks out of the door

When an experienced person leaves, everything they knew but never wrote down leaves with them. The business then pays to relearn it through mistakes.

Growth gets harder

You can't easily hand work to new people, take on more customers or open another site if everything depends on a few people remembering how things are done.

What good looks like

In a well-run business, the important tasks have a clear, agreed way of being done, and anyone can find it in seconds. New starters get up to speed quickly and confidently. The same questions stop coming to you. And the business keeps running smoothly when key people are away.

Getting there isn't about writing a thick manual nobody reads. It's about capturing the right things, in the right way, for how your team actually works. That's very different from one business to the next, and it's where most attempts go wrong.
