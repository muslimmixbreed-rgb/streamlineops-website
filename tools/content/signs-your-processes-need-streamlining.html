Processes rarely break all at once. They wear down quietly as the business grows, until everyone is busy but things still slip. Here are seven signs it's time to take a look, with a first step for each.

1. The same question gets asked again and again

"Where's the form for…?" "Who do I send this to?" If people keep asking, the answer isn't written down anywhere they can find it.

First step: keep a note of repeated questions for two weeks. The top three are your first SOPs.

2. Work stops when one person is away

If a holiday means invoices don't go out or orders don't get placed, that knowledge needs to move from a person into a process.

First step: ask that person to talk you through the task while you write down each step. That's a rough draft of an SOP.

3. Information is typed in more than once

Copying details from an email into a spreadsheet, then into an invoice, then into a diary takes time and creates errors at every step.

First step: pick one common job and list every place its details get entered. Ask which of those could be the single source.

4. Nobody is quite sure which version is right

"Price list FINAL v3 (2).xlsx" is a warning sign. When there are several copies, people make decisions on old information.

First step: agree one shared location and one owner for each key file, and archive the rest.

5. You hear about problems from customers

If a complaint is the first sign that something is late or wrong, you need an earlier warning.

First step: choose one simple measure, such as jobs past their due date, and check it every week.

6. Hand-offs are where things go missing

Most dropped balls happen between people or teams: sales to delivery, day shift to night shift, office to site.

First step: write down what "handed over" means for your most important hand-off: what information, in what format, and who confirms it's been received.

7. You're always firefighting

If most of your week goes on fixing today's problems, there's no time left to prevent tomorrow's. That cycle only breaks when someone steps back and looks at the process itself.

First step: block out one hour a week, every week, to fix the cause of one recurring problem.

Where to start

You don't need to fix everything. Pick the sign that costs you the most time or causes the most stress, and take the first step this week. Small, steady improvements add up.
