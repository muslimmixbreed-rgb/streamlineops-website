Framework 1 of 3 — Macro Analysis

PESTLE Analysis

PESTLE examines the six macro-environmental forces that shape the context your business operates in. These are factors you cannot control — but you must understand and plan around them.

🌍
PESTLE analyses the external environment — forces outside your business that could help or hinder it. Work through each factor in order. Claude analyses each one individually then gives you a PESTLE scorecard.

Framework 2 of 3 — Market Viability

Seven Domains Analysis

The Seven Domains Framework by John Mullins tests whether your business idea is viable across three levels: the macro market, your specific target segment, and your team's ability to execute.

🎯
The Seven Domains moves from the broad market down to your specific customers and finally to you as the founder. Each domain is rated Strong, Moderate, or Weak.

Framework 3 of 3 — Industry Analysis

Porter's Five Forces

Porter's Five Forces analyses the competitive structure of your industry. It reveals whether the industry itself is attractive to enter — regardless of how good your idea is.

⚔️
Five Forces examines competitive intensity. A high-intensity industry (many forces rated Strong threat) means lower profitability potential. A low-intensity industry is more attractive to enter.

Planning 90-Day Launch Plan ▾
Days 1–30 — Foundations
Get the basics right before you promote. Finalise your website, Google Business Profile, pricing, and enquiry process. Don't promote a business that isn't ready to convert.
Days 31–60 — Launch
Begin active promotional activity. Deploy your channel plan. Track enquiries from day one. Make sure follow-up is fast and consistent.
Days 61–90 — Review & Optimise
Pull the data. What's working? Cut what isn't. Double down on what is. Ask early customers for reviews and referrals.

PRINCE2 Project Brief

Build your PRINCE2 project brief

Once your analysis is complete, use these prompts with Claude to build a full PRINCE2 Project Brief. Copy each prompt and paste it into a new Claude conversation — include a summary of your analysis results for a tailored output.

💡
Tip: Before using these prompts, copy a summary of your analysis results from the Results tab and paste it at the start of your Claude conversation so the brief is tailored to your specific idea.
Step 1

Full project definition

Define the scope, objectives, constraints, and assumptions of your project.
"Build me a PRINCE2 Project Brief for [your idea]. Include: background and context, project objectives, in-scope and out-of-scope items, constraints, and assumptions."
Step 2

Business case

Justify the investment with expected benefits, costs, and revenue model options.
"Write the business case section of a PRINCE2 Project Brief for [your idea]. Include expected benefits, revenue model options with pros and cons, indicative costs, and investment appraisal."
Step 3

Risk register

Identify and document risks before they become problems.
"Produce a risk register for [your idea] as a table. Include: risk description, likelihood (High/Med/Low), impact (High/Med/Low), mitigation action, and owner for each risk."
Step 4

Product descriptions

Define what you are delivering and the acceptance criteria for each deliverable.
"Write PRINCE2-style Product Descriptions for the key deliverables of [your project]. For each product include: purpose, composition, format, and acceptance criteria."
Step 5

Full PID

Produce the complete Project Initiation Document — the single source of truth for your project.
"Using this Project Brief as a foundation, produce a full PRINCE2 Project Initiation Document (PID). Include quality strategy, communication plan, project controls, tolerances, and stage plans. Format as a Word document."
